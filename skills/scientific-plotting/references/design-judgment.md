# Design judgment examples

Use these examples to practice design judgment, not as a fixed template. They
come from a multi-revision review of a plotbook and its companion report. Each
one states what changed and why. The procedure is in
[plot production](plot-production.md) and the
[visual review protocol](../../render-review/references/visual-review.md).

Render selected pages of your own artifact at reading size before applying a
lesson. Contact sheets help locate structure; they cannot establish legend
readability. Test the rubric on unfamiliar examples too: recognizing these known
defects alone does not show that you will meet the standard.

## 1. Reassign space to the comparison that needs it

**Before:** one observable per page, with a separate row for each flavour and
separate columns for yield and shape. Comparable sources share axes, which
already improves on separate source pages, but each flavour consumes another row.

**After:** three observables grouped on one page, flavours overlaid, yield and
shape paired.

**Judgment:** overlay a dimension when doing so makes its relationship visible
without losing identifiable series. Spend the recovered space on unique evidence.
Do not infer that every future flavour or systematic family should be overlaid.
If uncertainty bands or nearly coincident curves obscure the intended comparison,
split the group again. An efficient page is not necessarily the fullest page.

## 2. Fix figure decoding when consolidation increases complexity

**Before:** fine dashed and dotted flavour curves and small hollow flavour
markers sit beside prominent combined points. In the legend, source and flavour
entries are interleaved rather than arranged as a matrix. Two separate issues
show up when inspected at normal size and enlarged: weak glyphs and a difficult
lookup order.

**After:** the legend is organized by source and state, and flavour, and the
line styles are simplified. A later revision corrects the still-hollow flavour
points to explicitly filled, equal-sized markers. A requested correction is not
proof it actually rendered.

**Judgment:** test a series-tracing task in both panels. Fine dashes and marker
shapes remain useful in other plots; they failed here in combination with dense
step curves, small placement and competing cues. The general solution is easier
decoding, not color-only as a permanent rule. Check main, ratio and legend glyphs.

## 3. Review the page, not only its component plots

**Before:** two conditional-comparison figures sit near the top of a page with
large unused space below. Small plot text and low page density coexist.

**After:** related conditional questions are grouped down fixed source columns,
with yield and shape on separate pages, and the page is used deliberately.

**Judgment:** choose what a row means, keep paired alternatives adjacent, then
use the page deliberately. A universal 2x3 mandate would miss other needs: a
complex transition label or a broad heatmap may deserve more width or height.
Do not meet a grid target by reducing text until the comparison is unreadable.

## 4. Make the effect visible throughout the inventory

A linear main axis can emphasize a peak while leaving a long tail difficult to
inspect. The scale review in the revision that followed extended beyond the
initially named examples to the whole catalogue and to newly admitted
populations.

**Judgment:** consider the question and the actual displayed support, including
uncertainties, before choosing linear, log or symlog. Check every affected
family. Changing the ratio reference between revisions was a separate scientific
comparison decision; it cannot be generalized as an aesthetic improvement.
Inspect final headroom after scales and limits: a late set_ylim had undone
earlier clearance.

## 5. Turn a checked record into a readable companion

**Before:** the report's first page combines source lineage, validation totals,
findings, reader routes and upstream processing before the argument is
established. The complete source had passed local copy corrections but still
needed a structural review. Correct sentences did not guarantee a clear
explanation.

**After:** the first page leads with the question, the finding and the evidence
identities. A short worked example of the comparison precedes the omission
table, and exact plotbook routes are given. Conventions and exhaustive lookup
have their own reference sections. The report stays seven pages, not a longer
tutorial.

**Judgment:** assume domain knowledge, not participation in the working
sessions. Explain the population before the ratio, introduce a method before its
result, and give recurring conventions one home. Reorganizing can supply the
missing reasoning without increasing length. Neither the page count nor these
section titles is a universal target.

## Use and limits

The progression changed coverage, spatial grouping, encoding, scale, reference
semantics and explanatory order. Those decisions require different checks. The
final artifacts were useful references, not perfect, and not independent
scientific acceptance. A good review can still find a justified improvement in
them.
