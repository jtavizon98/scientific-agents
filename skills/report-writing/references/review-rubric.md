# Review rubric for reports and plotbooks

A score for a review round, derived from the round's findings. Use it when a
report is graded while it is iterated on: a [council](../../council/SKILL.md)
brief names this file and each member returns the scores with the verdict.

The score decides nothing by itself. The verdict, the finding standard and the
round limit of the council stay as they are.

## What the brief adds

The generic part is this file. The project's brief supplies the rest:

- the study in a few sentences, the named reader and their stated feedback;
- the decisions already settled, which are carried out but not graded;
- the files under review with page counts and hashes;
- **the pages every member reads**, listed per file. Findings on other pages
  are reported under their own heading and do not enter the score. Without a
  fixed list two reviews read different pages and their scores cannot be
  compared.

## Criteria

Presentation, scored per document and for the set:

| # | Criterion |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Statements are correct and consistent with each other and with the evidence |
| 2 | Conclusions are calibrated to what the evidence shows |
| 3 | Structure is readable for the named reader |
| 4 | Plots stand on their own |
| 5 | Numbers and claims can be traced to sources |
| 6 | The work answers the readers' stated feedback |

Scope, scored apart and never added to the presentation total: the documents
state honestly what the study has not established. Evidence that is missing
counts under scope. A limit that is hidden or misstated is a presentation
finding under criterion 2.

## Findings first, then the score

A finding counts only if a run, a calculation or the page itself shows it, or
if it names the exact missing assumption. Anything else is an impression: list
it, do not score it. Give each finding its file and page, one criterion, and
one severity:

- **blocking**: a reader would be misled, or cannot use the document;
- **should-fix**: a reader is slowed or has to guess;
- **minor**: cosmetic.

Each criterion then takes its score from the findings assigned to it, on the
listed pages:

| Score | Meaning |
| ---: | --- |
| 3 | No finding |
| 2 | Minor findings only |
| 1 | At least one should-fix finding |
| 0 | At least one blocking finding |

The presentation total is the sum over the six criteria, out of 18. The set's
score for a criterion is the lowest score that criterion has in any document.

## Reading the score

- Compare a member with its own earlier round, never one member with another.
- A change no larger than the member's repeat spread is "no change". Measure
  the spread once per project: each member grades one frozen set twice on the
  same brief. Record the spread next to the scores. Until it is measured, the
  score is information only.
- A revision that rewrites pages brings new minor findings, so a flat score
  after a large rewrite is expected. Read the findings, not only the total.

## What a member returns

1. A table: one row per document and one for the set; columns for the six
   criteria, the total out of 18, and scope out of 3.
2. The verdict the council asks for.
3. Findings, most severe first, each with file, page, criterion, severity,
   evidence and a concrete fix.
4. Findings on pages outside the list.
5. Impressions and risks, apart from the findings.
