\section{Executive summary}

\begin{headline}
  One or two sentences: the section's result, before any detail.
\end{headline}

State the question, the main observed result, and the decision or review needed. Keep
numerical and physics conclusions qualified by the evidence below. Build the aids first
(plots, diagrams, commented equations, then lists and tables); prose connects them.

\section{Evidence}

Describe inputs, selections, methods, checks, and relevant figures or tables. Separate
direct observations from interpretation. For example:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Observed:} result directly supported by a named input or check.
  \item \textbf{Inference:} interpretation and its assumptions.
\end{itemize}

Shade rows so the eye can follow them; mark an outlier with colour and a symbol.

\section{Decisions}

List decisions already made, their owner, and the evidence that supports them. Every open
choice for the reader is a choice box:

\begin{choice}{what the reader decides}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \recommended\ The option, its reason, and its evidence (Table~\ref{tab:example}).
    \item \alternative\ The other option and what it would cost.
  \end{itemize}
\end{choice}

\section{Open questions}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Question, responsible owner, and the evidence needed to close it.
\end{itemize}

\section{Provenance}

Record repository, commit, configuration, input identifiers, execution host, date, and
reviewer. Do not embed credentials or unclassified internal text.

\section{Next steps}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Smallest next action, owner, and success criterion.
\end{enumerate}
