\chapter{Present the Work}\label{ch:talks}

A talk is an argument the audience hears once, in order, from the person who presents it.
The agent can draft the deck, but the presenter must be able to defend every frame. They
judge a deck by looking at it and learn whether they can defend it by giving the talk
aloud, so the agent shows a rendered skeleton first and prepares the rehearsal.

Beamer, the \LaTeX\ class used here, calls a slide a frame. A frame that reveals its
content step by step with \code{\\pause} becomes several PDF pages, one per overlay,
which share one page label. The render gate and the review inbox are those of
Chapter~\ref{ch:reports}.

\section{Settle the storyline on a skeleton}

\begin{headline}
  The agent writes the storyline and builds a skeleton: every main frame with its claim
  and a placeholder for its figure. The presenter confirms the rendered skeleton before
  any polish, and a later change of structure goes back to them as its own question.
\end{headline}

\section{Build the deck}

\begin{headline}
  Each frame title states a claim, the figures are the reviewed plotbook files, the diagrams
  are the study's asset files, and the talk adds no claim the written work does not
  support.
\end{headline}

\begin{table}[H]
  \small
  \begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{L{0.24\textwidth}L{0.18\textwidth}Y}
    \toprule
    Kind of talk & Size & What changes \\
    \midrule
    Own work (defence, colloquium, seminar) & set by the slot & Depth follows the audience;
    derivations and software details move to backup frames. \\
    \rowcolor{ctpSurface0!30}
    Re-cut of a deck & set by the slot & A new storyline; frames kept, moved to backup or removed are
    recorded. \\
    Someone else's paper & set by the slot & Claims kept apart from your assessment; a
    strengths and limits section; a rubric map when graded. \\
    \rowcolor{ctpSurface0!30}
    Group-meeting update & 3--8 frames & What changed, the plots that show it, the decisions
    needed; review status on the slides. \\
    Poster & A0 portrait & Blocks for question, method, result and take-away; larger font floors. \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabularx}
  \caption{Kinds of talk \cite[Kinds of talk]{skill:deck-production}.}
  \label{tab:talks-kinds}
\end{table}

Slide rules \cite[Slide rules]{skill:deck-production}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item one idea per frame, in the audience's words; at most 120 words set on a main frame;
  \item \code{\\pause} only where the audience must see the problem before the answer;
  \item a \code{\\scriptsize} source line under every figure or number from a published
        source; for an audience outside the group, no unpublished document named on a slide;
  \item every number generated from the artifact that owns it; no figure cropped of an axis;
  \item key numbers and the take-away in filled boxes; an equation's terms commented in place
        with \code{\\underbrace};
  \item backup frames linked from the frame that raises the question, and back;
  \item for a talk from your own study, the report's diagrams input from the study's
        \code{assets/} files with the same number macros, and the plotbook's navigation strip
        (the processing chain with the current step highlighted) in the footer of frames about
        one step \cite[Diagrams and navigation]{skill:deck-production}.
\end{itemize}

The template, a 16:9 Beamer deck and an A0 poster in the reports' palette, builds with
LuaLaTeX for the theme's Fira Sans. The presenter gets the source with the first draft
and their own edits are kept word for word; each request they make becomes a check that
runs on every build \cite[The presenter's source]{skill:deck-production}.

\section{Check and deliver}

\begin{headline}
  Every build passes the slides gate. Independent review runs at three milestones: the
  skeleton, the draft for the person who asked for the talk, and the final. The presenter
  rehearses aloud from a rehearsal pack, and what the rehearsal finds goes back into the deck.
\end{headline}

\begin{table}[H]
  \small
  \begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{L{0.3\textwidth}Y}
    \toprule
    Slides profile & Why \\
    \midrule
    Overlays grouped into frames by page label & Text hidden by \code{\\pause} is not a
    finding until the frame's last overlay. \\
    \rowcolor{ctpSurface0!30}
    No whitespace or density checks & An empty slide area is a design choice. \\
    Small text inside a placed figure fails on a main frame, is flagged on a backup &
    The audience must read a main frame's figure; text set on the slide keeps the fail
    floor everywhere. \\
    \rowcolor{ctpSurface0!30}
    More than 120 words set on a main frame fails & The audience reads or listens, not
    both; a frame that must stay dense is listed in the storyline. \\
    Text past the slide edge fails & The audience cannot read it. \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabularx}
  \caption{What the slides profile changes \cite[Slides]{skill:render-workflow}.}
  \label{tab:talks-gate}
\end{table}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \review\ The rehearsal pack gives, per frame, the points to hit and the terms to
        define, then the expected questions and the coach's instructions. A timed run the
        day before shows overruns and misstated numbers; an overrun is fixed by cutting
        frames \cite[The rehearsal pack]{skill:deck-production}.
  \item \review\ Claims review checks every number and claim against its source at each
        change of source. Drafts between milestones are recorded as checked by the author
        only.
  \item \review\ Visual review sends back to the source any frame with an unreadable claim, a
        plot without an axis, an
        element clashing with its surface, a style unlike frames of the same role, an unfinished
        layout or text too small for the room \cite[Build and review]{skill:deck-production}.
  \item \evidence\ The PowerPoint copy, when the room needs one, holds one picture per
        slide; edit the source, not the \code{.pptx}.
  \item \gap\ Uploading or sending the deck is the presenter's step, never the agent's.
  \item \evidence\ Late requests on the day of the talk are applied directly; a closing
        record afterwards names the file presented, what changed that day and what went
        unchecked \cite[The day of the talk]{skill:deck-production}.
\end{itemize}
